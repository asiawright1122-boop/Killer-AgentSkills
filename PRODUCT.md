# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Killer-Skills serves developers, AI coding-agent power users, SEO visitors, and teams evaluating reusable AI agent skills before installing them into Claude Code, Cursor, Windsurf, VS Code, and other IDE environments. They usually arrive with one of three intents: find a trustworthy skill for a concrete workflow, compare curated categories before installing anything, or understand whether this directory is safer than browsing raw GitHub repositories.

## Product Purpose

Killer-Skills is the public discovery and installation entry point for AI agent skills, MCP servers, and IDE workflow tools. The site must turn a large open-source corpus into confident decisions: category-led browsing, evidence-first collections, one-command install paths, and public trust surfaces that explain the review pipeline. Success means visitors can remember the value proposition, choose a category or curated path quickly, and understand why installing through Killer-Skills is safer than copying instructions from unreviewed repositories.

## Brand Personality

Engineering-led, sharp, and accountable. The brand should feel like a public trust layer built by people who actually run the checks they describe. The redesign is allowed to replace the previous hard-edged terminal/brutalist identity entirely; the durable identity is classification clarity, review evidence, and confident installation paths rather than a specific visual style. Avoid generic SaaS polish, decorative AI gradients, and vague "trusted" claims that are not backed by visible evidence.

## Anti-references

Do not copy CocoLoop's mascot or personality system; compete with clearer structure and stronger engineering proof. Do not ship a flat raw directory as the primary experience. Do not use generic AI landing-page tropes such as gradient text, nested card grids, repeated tiny section eyebrows, fake trust badges, decorative grid backgrounds, or metric-heavy hero sections. Do not make the site look like a dashboard unless the surface is actually an app workflow.

## Design Principles

1. Lead with classification, not volume: categories, rankings, and curated paths should help users choose before the full directory broadens the search.
2. Make trust visible: safety checks, schema review, opt-out policy, takedown policy, and CI gates should become public surfaces rather than hidden implementation details.
3. Keep claims concrete: every security or quality statement needs a visible source, rule, policy, or workflow behind it.
4. Rebuild the identity around trust flow: visual hierarchy should make classification, review, and installation feel sequential, calm, and inspectable without relying on the old fluorescent terminal/brutalist language.
5. Design for multilingual authority: the information architecture must work in Chinese and English first, then survive longer strings across all shipped locales.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG AA for public pages. Body copy, placeholders, buttons, and badges must have sufficient contrast in light and dark modes. Keyboard focus must remain visible. Motion must respect reduced-motion preferences and must not gate content visibility. Responsive layouts must prevent text overflow in all shipped locales, especially long German, Russian, Arabic, and translated category names.
